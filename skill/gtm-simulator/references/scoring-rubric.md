# Scoring rubric

Interest score = how likely this persona is to take a next step (reply, take a meeting, forward it
to the person who decides) after reading the message cold, in the middle of a normal work week.

| Score | Meaning | What the persona would actually do |
|---|---|---|
| 85–100 | Urgent fit | Replies today. The message names their exact problem and they believe it. |
| 70–84 | Strong interest | Takes the meeting or forwards it with a note. |
| 55–69 | Curious | Might click. Needs proof before giving time. |
| 40–54 | Indifferent | Reads it and moves on. Nothing wrong, nothing for them. |
| 25–39 | Skeptical | Actively doubts the claim, or it's aimed at someone else's problem. |
| 0–24 | Negative | It raises a red flag: surveillance, cost, a threat to their role, or obvious hype. |

## Calibration

- A realistic panel for decent B2B copy centers around 45–55, with a long tail on both sides.
  If your mean is above 65, re-check for flattery.
- Score from the persona's pains and trigger, not from the quality of the writing. Beautiful copy
  aimed at the wrong pain still scores low.
- Skepticism pulls the score down and widens it. A persona at skepticism 0.8 needs proof in the
  message itself (a number, a customer, a mechanism) to clear 60.
- Change fatigue is resistance. Messages that sound like a new rollout, a new login or more
  oversight lose points with high change-fatigue personas, even when the pain matches.
- Role stakes override pain. A broker with a high premium pain may still score low if the message
  reads like it goes around them. A worker with high injury pain may still score low if the
  message sounds like cameras watching them.

## Confidence

- **high**: the message clearly hits or clearly misses this persona's top pains.
- **medium**: it touches their pains indirectly.
- **low**: hard to tell. The score is a guess. (The dashboard widens the Monte Carlo noise for
  low-confidence scores.)
